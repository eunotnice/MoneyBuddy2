# Carbon Footprint Equivalency Method

## Overview
Carbon footprint calculators estimate greenhouse gas emissions associated with activities such as energy consumption, transportation, and purchasing behavior.

These emissions are commonly expressed as **kilograms of CO₂ equivalent (kg CO₂e)**.

## Purpose of Equivalency Metrics
To help users better understand environmental impact, some tools convert emissions into familiar comparisons.

Examples include:

- number of trees needed to absorb the emissions
- distance driven by a typical car
- electricity usage equivalents

## Tree Equivalency
Tree equivalency estimates how many trees would be required to absorb a certain amount of carbon emissions over time.

Different environmental organizations use different assumptions depending on:

- tree species
- growth stage
- climate
- time horizon

Because of these factors, tree equivalencies should be interpreted as **approximate educational comparisons**, not exact offsets.

## Carbon Tracking in Applications
Applications may estimate carbon emissions based on spending categories.

Examples:

- transport spending → estimated fuel usage
- electricity bills → estimated electricity emissions

These calculations rely on **average emission factors** rather than precise individual measurements.

## Limitations
Carbon footprint estimates are approximations because:

- spending does not perfectly reflect actual consumption
- emission factors vary by region
- supply chain impacts are complex

Therefore carbon reports should be interpreted as **educational indicators rather than precise measurements**.
