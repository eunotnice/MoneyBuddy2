# 50/30/20 Budgeting Rule

## Overview
The 50/30/20 budgeting rule is a simple guideline for allocating personal income into three categories: needs, wants, and savings. It is commonly used as a starting framework for managing personal finances.

## Basic Allocation
The rule suggests dividing after-tax income as follows:

- **50% for Needs**
- **30% for Wants**
- **20% for Savings or Debt Repayment**

This allocation is a guideline rather than a strict rule.

## Needs
Needs include essential expenses required for daily living and financial stability.

Examples:
- Housing and rent
- Groceries
- Utilities
- Transport
- Healthcare
- Education
- Basic services

## Wants
Wants include non-essential lifestyle spending.

Examples:
- Entertainment
- Shopping
- Travel
- Dining out
- Subscriptions

## Savings and Financial Goals
Savings may include:

- Emergency funds
- Long-term savings
- Debt repayment
- Retirement contributions
- Low-risk financial products such as fixed deposits

## Limitations
The 50/30/20 rule may not fit every financial situation. Actual allocations depend on:

- income level
- cost of living
- family commitments
- debt obligations
- financial goals

In some regions or income levels, essential expenses may exceed 50%.

## Use in Financial Tools
Budgeting applications may use the 50/30/20 rule as a **reference guideline** to help users understand their spending distribution. It should not be treated as strict financial advice.
