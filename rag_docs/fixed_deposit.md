# Fixed Deposits: Safety and Trade-Offs

## What is a Fixed Deposit
A fixed deposit (FD) is a financial product offered by banks where money is deposited for a fixed period of time. In return, the bank pays interest at a predetermined rate.

## Key Characteristics

### Capital Preservation
Fixed deposits are generally designed to preserve the deposited capital.

### Predictable Returns
Interest rates are typically fixed at the time of deposit, providing predictable returns.

### Fixed Term
Funds are usually locked for a specific period such as:

- 3 months
- 6 months
- 12 months
- longer terms

## Liquidity Considerations
Accessing funds before the maturity date may involve:

- reduced interest
- early withdrawal penalties
- restrictions depending on the bank

## Common Uses
People often use fixed deposits for:

- conservative savings strategies
- storing emergency funds
- short-term savings goals

## Limitations
Fixed deposits may have limitations compared to other financial instruments.

Examples include:

- lower returns compared to higher-risk investments
- limited flexibility during the fixed term
- potential penalties for early withdrawal

## Role in Personal Finance
Fixed deposits are often used as part of a **risk-management strategy** within personal finance. They are typically considered a conservative financial product.
